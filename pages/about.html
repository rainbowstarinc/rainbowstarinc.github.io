True Story

Here I Am

My name is Joy, and my path into healing began long before I had words for it. A close friend in graduate school was the first to name what she saw: wherever I went, children would stop and simply stare — deeply still, quietly magnetized. She recognized something I hadn't yet claimed in myself, and pointed me toward child psychology. I followed.

In grad school I found the modalities that would shape everything: family systems therapy, Jungian sand tray and world play, pet-assisted therapy. From the moment I encountered animals as part of the therapeutic field, I knew they belonged in my work — though I didn't yet have the inner resource to reach them.

Then the accident happened. A car crash interrupted my practice, my motherhood, my speech, my body. What followed were years of surgery and nerve pain — and inside all of that, a much more profound journey: learning to truly inhabit myself. Through somatic work, yoga therapy, pelvic floor healing, Rolfing, craniosacral therapy, and Nia — an integrative dance practice born in Portland — I found my way back. Nia gave me my first real experience of bliss in a body that had known mostly pain. I learned that pleasure is not separate from healing. It is healing.

Energy arrived through touch. A dear friend who discovered it alongside me. The Reiki practitioners who worked on my body during recovery. And one quiet night, my hands resting on my sleeping son — I began to see pictures of what was moving in him, and felt waves of ecstasy and deep love move through me. I didn't have a name for it yet. I simply knew.

When a brain injury surfaced during that same time, my words went with it. Speaking — which had always been my greatest strength — became unreachable. I prayed for help, and found some old paints. What came out of me was a shock: color, grace, ease. A language that needed nothing from my fractured speech. Painting became how my inner self communicated — with me, and through me, with others. I've learned to trust those urgent painting impulses. The messages are often larger than I am.

I am a channel. A multidimensional being of light, embodied in this particular, extraordinary frame. I am loving awareness. I am stepping into courage and joy — and I am committed, fully, to sharing these gifts.

The quieter you become, the more you can hear.

— Ram Dass
